What This Model Surfaces
Three things that became clear while building this — observations worth stress-testing against real platform data.
01
Apply friction selects against quality, not just quantity. When friction is high, the candidates who push through aren't the most qualified — they're the least selective. This means a high application count on a high-friction channel is a warning sign, not a success metric. The damage happens before the first click is tracked.
02
Spray-apply behaviour and bot behaviour are indistinguishable at the surface level. Both inflate application count while collapsing hire rate. But they need different responses — friction reduction fixes spray-apply, fraud suppression fixes bots. A platform that treats them identically will fix the wrong thing. This model doesn't solve that problem, but it flags that the distinction matters.
03
The decay curve is a lagging signal. By the time CPA rises enough to trigger reallocation, the fraud and recirculation causing it started 2–3 weeks earlier. If the behavioural chain above follows a predictable pattern by channel and role type, it may be possible to flag the entry conditions for decay before spend is committed — not just respond to the outcome.

Methodology & Honest Limitations

This is a proof-of-concept built on synthetic data. Decay rate estimates are derived from three sources: Joveo's MOJO platform brochure (which identifies apply fraud and spam as the primary drivers of poor application-to-hire conversion); Joveo's Auto Insurer case study (which showed a 221% improvement in click-to-apply simply by reducing form friction — confirming that friction is a measurable quality signal); and Joveo's Inergroup case study (which highlighted that without centralized visibility, quality decay goes undetected until CPA has already spiked).

The behavioural chain — friction → frustration → spray-apply → audience exhaustion → fraud entry — is a hypothesis about the mechanism behind the numbers, not a validated model. The fraud entry weeks and decay rates per channel are estimated, not measured. Real validation would require: per-candidate application velocity data, JD read-time before apply, and actual source quality scores over campaign duration from a live platform.

The core question this prototype is asking: if these behavioural patterns are consistent enough to be predictable, could they be used to trigger automated spend suppression before quality collapse — rather than after? That's what I'd want to explore with real data.
